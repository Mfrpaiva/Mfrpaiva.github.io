🌟Starting to lead an independent research agenda

Project: **Integral Fractionation and Conversion of Lignocellulosic Biomass Using Molten Salt Hydrates and Innovative Catalysts**

This nationally funded project marks the beginning of my independent research line in catalytic biomass valorization. The project develops integrated technologies capable of converting lignocellulosic biomass into high-value products through:<br>
-Molten Salt Hydrates (MSH) as unconventional reaction media;<br>
-Heterogeneous acid catalysis;<br>
-Photocatalytic and hybrid catalytic systems;<br>
-Continuous reactor configurations;<br>
-Emerging biocatalytic routes.

In addition to conventional feedstocks (sugarcane bagasse, pine, eucalyptus), we investigate underexplored biomass residues such as açaí seeds and water hyacinth, transforming environmental liabilities into renewable chemical platforms.

This work advances:<br>
-Second-generation biorefinery concepts;<br>
-Mild and energy-efficient processing;<br>
-Integrated valorization of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin;<br>
-National strategies for bioeconomy development.

Beyond technology, this project represents leadership in:<br>
-Training undergraduate researchers (always trying to find ways to bring in more students and build a team);<br>
-Building interdisciplinary collaborations;<br>
-Strengthening Brazil’s innovation ecosystem<br>

✈️ <strong style="color: #4a9fd8;">2024 – 2025</strong> (Post-Doc)
Project: **Renewable Hydrocarbons for Sustainable Aviation**
(CNPq – Fuel of the Future & Brazilian Hydrogen Initiative)

In alignment with Brazil’s strategic energy programs, this project explored innovative routes for producing precursors to Sustainable Aviation Fuels (SAFs). Two lignin isolation strategies were systematically compared:<br>
-Organosolv fractionation;<br>
-Molten Salt Hydrate systems (e.g., LiBr·3H₂O);

The MSH route enabled:<br>
-High-purity lignin isolation (>99%); <br>
-Lower structural condensation; Enhanced downstream reactivity;<br>

Isolated lignins were subjected to Reductive Catalytic Depolymerization (RCD) under hydrogen atmosphere, generating:
-Guaiacol and syringol;<br>
-Alkylphenols;<br>
-Partially hydrogenated aromatics.<br>
*These compounds serve as promising renewable precursors for aviation fuels.* 

This project strengthened the bridge between lignin chemistry and real-world fuel applications, positioning catalytic biomass valorization within the framework of global decarbonization.<br>

🎓 <strong style="color: #4a9fd8;">2021 – 2024</strong> – Université de Lille (France)
**PhD Research – Integrated Biorefinery Concepts**

My doctoral research proposed a paradigm shift: *Integrating biomass fractionation and catalytic upgrading into a single process.*

Using inorganic molten salt hydrates as solvents, I demonstrated:

-Low-temperature dissolution of cellulose and hemicellulose;<br>
-In situ catalytic conversion to platform molecules;<br>
-Isolation of high-purity lignins;<br>
-Catalytic depolymerization routes;<br>
-Ignition-delay studies of lignin-derived hydrocarbons.

This work uniquely connected: **Catalysis → Biomass Chemistry → Reaction Engineering → Combustion Kinetics**

By studying ignition reactivity of deoxygenated lignin derivatives, the research extended beyond chemical production into fuel performance evaluation, contributing to the feasibility assessment of renewable hydrocarbons in transportation systems.<br>

🔬 <strong style="color: #4a9fd8;">2017 – 2021</strong>
*Engineering Acid Catalysts for Sustainable Transformations*

During this period, my work focused on designing and tuning heterogeneous catalysts, particularly:
-Heteropolyacid-modified zeolites (HPW, HSiW);<br>
-Faujasite (Y), BEA, and FER structures;<br>
-Acid site engineering and dispersion control.

Catalytic applications included:<br>
-Oleic acid esterification (>70% conversion, no leaching);<br>
-Ethanol dehydration to ethylene (100% selectivity at 300 °C);<br>
-Adsorption of priority PAHs (EPA-listed pollutants);<br>
-D,L-lactic acid polymerization;<br>
-Renewable polymers production.

Through advanced characterization (XRD, MAS-NMR, Py-FTIR, N₂ physisorption, TPD), I investigated structure–activity relationships and catalyst stability, strengthening my expertise in rational catalyst design.<br>

**Research Philosophy**

Catalysis is not merely a reaction accelerator.
**It is a strategic tool for societal transformation.**

My research seeks to catalyze more than molecules — *it seeks to catalyze systems, ideas, people, transitions, and possibilities.* 
By integrating catalyst design, process intensification, and fuel performance evaluation, I aim to contribute to a new generation of biorefineries capable of delivering renewable fuels and chemicals with tangible technological and environmental impact. Beyond scientific advancement, I believe that true catalysis also happens at the human level. Through mentorship, collaboration, and interdisciplinary dialogue, I strive to empower students and young researchers to become transformative agents themselves. In this sense, my work is not only about accelerating reactions, but about accelerating potential — fostering scientific curiosity, resilience, and innovation in the next generation. Because to catalyze is not only to transform matter. It is to activate change.
